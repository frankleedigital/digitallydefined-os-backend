The 5-Step Protection Playbook

Welcome to your operational manual for a protected digital legacy. As a digital landlord, your goal is simple: Build once, protect fiercely, earn forever.

Module 1: Trademarking vs. Copyrighting Faceless Content

When you build an anonymous brand, the brand name and content are your only visible assets. You must know how to shield them.

  • Copyright: Automatically protects your original content (blog posts, Notion templates, digital guides) the moment you hit publish.
  • Trademark: Protects your brand identifier (domain name, logo, podcast title).
The 3-Step Database Check (Before Buying a Domain): Never buy a domain without checking USPTO and WIPO databases. A quick check for your intended brand name in Class 35 (Advertising/Business Management) avoids costly rebrands.

Module 2: Structuring Digital Leasing Agreements (Rank-and-Rent)

In Rank-and-Rent, ownership is everything. You are leasing the leads, not the asset itself. Your contracts must reflect this explicitly to protect your digital property.

  • The Non-Interference Clause: State explicitly that the client has no ownership of the domain, the hosting, or the content.
  • The Suspension Protocol: Write into your agreement that if payment is late, you may pause lead routing after notice and a cure period — in line with applicable law. A sample template is included below.
LEGAL ALERT: THE "JOINT VENTURE" TRAP
Never use the terms "partner" or "joint venture" with local contractors. This can imply shared ownership. You are a Digital Landlord; they are a Tenant. Use "Service Agreement." This is general guidance, not legal advice — confirm with an attorney.

Module 3: FTC Disclosures & Privacy Architectures

  • Dead-End Email Architecture: Create separate, encrypted emails for domain registrations unlinked from personal accounts.
  • FTC Disclosures: For faceless affiliate marketing, your disclosure must be clear, conspicuous, and above the fold.

Module 4: Digital Asset Valuation & Tax Tracking

Track the lifecycle of digital equity across three categories: Income Tax (Rent), Capital Gains (Flipping), and Cost Basis (Hosting/Tools). Work with a CPA on your specific treatment.

Module 5: Sovereign Liquidation (Exit Readiness)

A buyer cannot purchase "you." They can only purchase an autonomous system. By keeping the brand faceless and documenting your Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), you create a turnkey asset ready for sale. Site-flip multiples vary widely with profit level, brand strength, and due diligence — typical service-business multiples are roughly 3x–8x annual profit; document everything so a buyer can underwrite it.

1. Parties & Effective Date

This Service Agreement ("Agreement") is effective as of ______ ("Effective Date") between:

  1. Service Provider ("Landlord"): LLC/entity name, with principal contact email, registered in state.
  2. Client ("Tenant"): Business name, operating at address, represented by name/title.

The parties agree to the following terms for the provision of lead-generation and website services related to the digital property identified in Exhibit A.

2. Scope of Services

Landlord will provide the following services for the Digital Property:

  • Ownership, hosting, and maintenance of the domain: domain.com
  • Local SEO and content upkeep
  • Routing of inbound inquiries (leads) to Tenant's designated contact: phone/email

Tenant will not own, control, or access the underlying domain, hosting, source files, or content by virtue of this Agreement.

3. Ownership of the Digital Property

3.1 Landlord retains 100% ownership of the domain name, website, content, analytics, and all related intellectual property (collectively, the "Digital Property").

3.2 This Agreement grants Tenant a non-exclusive, non-transferable right to receive the leads generated by the Digital Property during the Term. No ownership, license, or interest in the Digital Property itself is transferred to Tenant.

3.3 Upon termination, all rights to the Digital Property revert to Landlord without further obligation.

4. Fees & Payment

4.1 Tenant will pay Landlord $___ per month, invoiced monthly in advance on day of month.

4.2 Payments are due by the 15th of each month and may be made by method.

4.3 A late fee of ___% applies to amounts unpaid after a ___-day grace period, to the extent permitted by applicable law.

5. Term & Renewal

This Agreement begins on the Effective Date and continues for term length, e.g., 12 months ("Initial Term"), and renews automatically for successive period unless either party gives ___ days written notice of non-renewal.

6. Suspension & Termination

6.1 If a payment is more than ___ days past due, Landlord may provide written notice and, after a ___-day cure period, pause lead routing until the account is current.

6.2 Either party may terminate for material breach upon ___ days written notice if the breach is not cured. Any suspension or termination must comply with applicable law.

6.3 On termination, Landlord may resume or redirect the Digital Property to other clients without liability to Tenant.

7. Non-Interference & Restrictions

7.1 Tenant shall not attempt to acquire, register, or divert the domain or Digital Property.

7.2 Tenant acknowledges Landlord's ownership and shall not create confusion about the source of the leads or the Digital Property.

8. Confidentiality

Each party will keep the other's non-public business information confidential for duration and use it only to perform this Agreement.

9. Independent Contractor

Landlord is an independent contractor. Nothing in this Agreement creates an employment, partnership, agency, or joint-venture relationship. The parties are landlord and tenant for the Digital Property.

10. Liability & Indemnification

10.1 Landlord's total liability under this Agreement is limited to fees paid in the ___ months preceding the claim, to the extent permitted by law.

10.2 Tenant will defend and indemnify Landlord against claims arising from Tenant's own business activities, including lead follow-up and service delivery.

11. Governing Law & Disputes

This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of ______. Disputes will first be addressed through good-faith negotiation, then mediation/arbitration or courts in ____________.
